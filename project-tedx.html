Overview

What is this project?

TEDxBangkokYouth 2026 is an independently organized TED event for youth in Bangkok. The theme "Thaigredient" centers Thai cultural identity as the core ingredient of new ideas. This website serves two audiences at once — attendees looking to register and explore talks, and the staff team managing operations behind the scenes.

Research

Understanding the problem

The operations team was buried in manual work — paper registration forms, scattered documents, and no central place to track ticket purchases. The goal was to reduce that friction by centralizing everything into one platform.

Target Users
Youth interested in attending talks, and TEDx staff managing operations and ticket verification.
Core Problem
Paper-based registration and scattered workflows created heavy operational burden for the Oper team.
Design Goal
One platform for ticket purchase, registration, and staff verification — reducing manual document handling.

Design Process

How it came together

1
Define scope & user flows
Mapped out two distinct user journeys — attendee (browse → register → buy ticket) and staff (dashboard → verify → manage).
2
Wireframing
Low-fidelity wireframes to establish layout and information hierarchy before committing to visual direction.
3
Visual design
Built a playful, vibrant UI inspired by Thai visual culture — bold colors, expressive typography, and fun graphic elements that match the event's KV.
4
Prototype & review
Built interactive prototype in Figma and reviewed flows with the team for operational accuracy.

Key Design Decision

Why it looks the way it does

The KV was bold and vibrant — so the UI had to match. Instead of a safe, minimal event website, I leaned into playful Thai visual elements to give the site its own character.

The design reflects the event's spirit, not just its information.

Reflection

What I learned

This was my first experience designing within a professional team structure — working alongside operations, marketing, and content teams with real deadlines and real stakeholders. It taught me how design decisions ripple into other departments, and how to communicate design intent clearly to non-designers. Working within an organized system, not just a classroom project, changed how I think about UX.
