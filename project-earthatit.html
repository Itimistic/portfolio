Overview

Never get lost between classes again

The IT faculty at KMITL spans multiple floors and dozens of rooms. When class schedules change or rooms shift, students scramble. Earth@IT solves this by linking your class schedule to a live map — calculating the fastest route from your current location to your next classroom, accounting for stairs, elevators, and nearby rooms.

Research

A problem we all had

This project came from personal experience — being a student in a building where rooms aren't intuitively numbered and class changes happen at the last minute. The confusion is real and frequent.

Target Users
Students and staff navigating the multi-floor IT faculty building at KMITL between classes.
Core Problem
Room numbers and floor layouts are confusing, especially when classes change rooms at short notice.
Design Goal
A navigation app that links your timetable to the building map and gives turn-by-turn indoor directions.

Key Features

What it does

Timetable integration
Links to your class schedule so the app knows where you need to be and when.
Distance calculation
Calculates the shortest route from current location to the destination classroom.
Stairs & lift guidance
Specifies whether to take stairs or elevator, based on floor and accessibility.
Nearby room info
Shows adjacent rooms in the corridor so you can orient yourself quickly.

Design Process

Mapping the building before designing the app

1
Physical research
Walked the building floors, mapped room numbering logic, identified pain points like ambiguous staircases and unmarked lifts.
2
Information architecture
Structured how rooms, floors, and routes would be stored and queried — the data model behind the navigation.
3
UI design in Figma
Designed map views, route displays, and timetable linking screens with clarity as the priority.
4
Build & test in-building
Implemented and tested the navigation logic on real floors of the faculty building.

Key Design Decision

Navigation tied to the timetable

Instead of making users search for a room manually, the app reads the timetable and already knows where you need to go next. Navigation becomes proactive, not reactive.

The best interface is one that already knows the answer.

Reflection

What I learned

Earth@IT was more complex than it looked from the outside. Mapping a physical space into a navigable data structure required thinking like both a designer and a systems thinker. It was also the first time I did research by literally walking the environment rather than sitting at a desk — and that made the design better.
